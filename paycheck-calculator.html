Paycheck Calculator

Estimate take-home pay from your gross paycheck using a simple deduction rate you choose. See net pay per check and annualized totals for weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, or monthly pay.

Who this calculator is for

Use this when you want a quick sense of net pay after taxes and other deductions—comparing job offers, planning a monthly budget, or checking what a raise might mean in take-home dollars. It is intentionally simple: you supply the combined deduction rate instead of filing status and full tax tables.

It is not an official IRS or state withholding calculator and should not be used as tax advice.

How the calculation works

Net pay ≈ Gross × (1 − deduction rate)

Enter gross pay for one paycheck and an estimated percentage for taxes and other deductions (for example 22% or 28%). The tool multiplies gross by one minus that rate. It can also annualize using your pay frequency (52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 semimonthly, or 12 monthly checks).

Real paystubs may subtract federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, state and local tax, retirement contributions, and health premiums separately. Bundling them into one rate keeps the estimate fast but less precise.

Worked examples

Example 1. Gross $2,000 biweekly with a 22% combined rate → net about $1,560 per check, or roughly $40,560 per year if paid 26 times.

Example 2. Gross $3,500 monthly at 25% → net about $2,625 per month, about $31,500 per year.

Example 3. Gross $900 weekly at 20% → net about $720 weekly. Raise the rate if you contribute heavily to a 401(k) or pay large pre-tax benefits.

Choosing a deduction rate

Many people start between 15% and 35% depending on income, state tax, and benefits. Look at a recent paystub: divide total deductions by gross pay to get a personal rate. W-4 elections, bonuses, and multiple jobs can change withholding a lot from paycheck to paycheck.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official tax calculator?

No. It uses the rate you enter and does not apply federal, state, or local tax tables, credits, or exact withholding rules.

What tax rate should I use?

A rough combined rate of 20–30% is common for ballpark estimates, but your real rate depends on filing status, deductions, state, and benefits.

Does it include 401(k) or health insurance?

Not as separate lines. Increase the deduction percentage to approximate those amounts.

Why doesn’t this match my paystub?

Employers use IRS and state methods, pre-tax elections, and garnishments this tool does not model. Treat the result as a planning estimate only.

Can I use it for hourly workers?

Yes—enter gross for the pay period (hours × rate, plus overtime if you already calculated it). Or use the overtime and time-card tools first.

Is my data stored?

No. Calculations run in your browser. See the privacy policy.
