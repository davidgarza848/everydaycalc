QUICK GUIDES

How these calculators work

Short explanations so you understand the numbers—not only the result. Open any money tool for worked examples and FAQs on a dedicated page.

Paycheck estimates

Enter gross pay and an estimated tax/deduction rate. Net ≈ gross × (1 − rate). Real take-home depends on filing status, state tax, benefits, and retirement contributions. Use the paycheck calculator for a starting point, then compare with a pay stub.

Loan & car payments

We use the standard fixed-rate formula: payment = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of payments. Fees, insurance, and variable rates are not included. See the loan and car loan tools.

Mortgage & amortization

Mortgage payment estimates use the same fixed-payment math as other loans, usually over 15–30 years. The amortization schedule shows how early payments are mostly interest. Taxes, insurance, HOA, and PMI are separate.

Refinance break-even

Compare your current payment with a new rate and term, then divide closing costs by monthly savings to estimate months to break even. A lower payment is not always cheaper overall if the term stretches. Use the refinance calculator for a simplified view.

Overtime & schedules

Regular hours use your base rate; overtime uses the multiplier you enter (often 1.5×). The schedule tool subtracts unpaid breaks and can handle overnight shifts. Labor rules vary—confirm with your employer.

Compound interest

A = P(1 + r/n)n·t. More frequent compounding grows a balance slightly faster. This tool does not model monthly deposits, fees, or taxes. Helpful for rough savings growth questions.

BMI (screening only)

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Categories follow common WHO adult ranges. BMI does not measure body fat or diagnose health conditions—treat it as a simple screening number.

Shopping & everyday math

Sales tax, discounts, tips, percentages, grades, and unit conversion are quick utilities. They are intentionally simple. For money decisions with long-term impact, prefer the dedicated loan and mortgage pages.

GOOD TO KNOW

Frequently asked questions

Are these calculators free?

Yes. You can use the calculators without creating an account. The site is supported by advertising so the tools can stay free to use.

Are the results official financial or tax advice?

No. Results are estimates for general information. Paycheck estimates use the tax rate you enter and do not calculate actual federal, state, or local tax liability. Loan and mortgage results assume a fixed rate and regular payments and exclude many fees and insurance costs.

Why do money tools have longer pages?

Borrowing, refinancing, and paycheck planning affect real budgets. Those pages include examples, limitations, and FAQs so the estimate is easier to interpret—not only a single number on a blank page.

How does EverydayCalc make money?

The site uses Google AdSense advertising to help cover costs and keep the tools free. No account is required to use the calculators. See the privacy policy for how ads and cookies work.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes. The layout adapts to phones, tablets, and desktop screens. You can bookmark individual calculator pages from the address bar.

Do you store the numbers I enter?

Calculator math runs in your browser. The values you type into a tool are not sent to EverydayCalc servers as part of computing the result. Advertising partners may use cookies as described in the privacy policy.

How do I report a mistake or request a tool?

Email hello@evrydaycalc.com with the calculator name, what you entered, and what you expected. We read messages and use them to improve the site.
