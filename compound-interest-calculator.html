Compound Interest Calculator

Project how a starting balance can grow when interest is added to the balance and then earns interest itself. Choose compounding frequency and time horizon.

Who this calculator is for

Use it for savings accounts, CDs, or simplified investment growth when you want a clear future-value estimate. It does not model market volatility, fees, taxes, or regular monthly deposits (unless you approximate by adjusting the starting amount).

How the calculation works

A = P(1 + r/n)n·t

P is the starting principal, r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, n is compounds per year, and t is time in years. More frequent compounding produces slightly higher growth for the same nominal rate.

Worked examples

Example 1. $10,000 at 5% compounded monthly for 10 years grows to about $16,470 (about $6,470 interest).

Example 2. Same balance at 5% compounded annually for 10 years grows to about $16,289—a bit less than monthly compounding.

Example 3. $5,000 at 4% monthly for 5 years → about $6,105.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include monthly deposits?

Not in the basic formula. It grows a single starting amount. Future versions may add contributions.

Is the rate APY or APR?

Enter the nominal annual rate your account uses; compounding frequency is selected separately.

Are investment returns guaranteed?

No. Markets fluctuate. This is a mathematical projection, not a promise of returns.

What about taxes and fees?

Not deducted. Real accounts may withhold tax or charge fees that reduce growth.

Why does daily compounding help only a little?

Beyond monthly compounding, the extra growth is usually small at typical savings rates.
