Mortgage Payment Calculator

Estimate the fixed monthly principal-and-interest payment on a home loan. Enter the loan amount, annual interest rate, and term to see your payment, total interest, and total amount paid over the life of the loan.

Who this calculator is for

Use this tool when you are comparing home prices, checking whether a listed rate fits your budget, or estimating how term length changes the monthly payment. It is designed for fixed-rate loans where the rate and payment stay the same each month.

It is not a loan approval, a Good Faith Estimate, or a substitute for a lender’s official disclosure. Closing costs, escrow, and mortgage insurance can change what you actually pay.

How the calculation works

The calculator uses the standard amortization formula for a fixed-rate loan paid monthly:

Payment = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1)

Here P is the loan principal (amount borrowed), r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the number of monthly payments (years × 12). If the rate is zero, the payment is simply principal divided by the number of months.

Each payment covers interest on the remaining balance first; the rest reduces principal. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal. This page shows the level monthly payment and the total interest if you keep that payment for the full term.

Worked examples

Example 1 — 30-year fixed. Loan amount $350,000, rate 6.5%, term 30 years. Monthly principal and interest is about $2,213. Over 360 payments you would pay roughly $447,000 in interest and about $797,000 total (principal plus interest). Taxes and insurance are not included.

Example 2 — 15-year fixed. Same $350,000 at 6.0% for 15 years. The monthly payment rises (often near $2,950), but total interest drops sharply compared with a 30-year term because you repay principal faster.

Example 3 — smaller loan. $200,000 at 6.5% for 30 years yields a monthly payment near $1,264. Use the live calculator to adjust rate and term for your scenario.

What is not included

  • Property taxes and homeowners insurance (escrow)
  • Private mortgage insurance (PMI) or mortgage insurance premiums
  • HOA or condo fees
  • Origination fees, points, and closing costs
  • Adjustable-rate mortgages (ARMs) and interest-only periods

Your full housing payment is often higher than principal and interest alone. Ask your lender for a complete breakdown.

Tips for using the estimate

Compare the same loan amount across 15- and 30-year terms to see the tradeoff between monthly payment and lifetime interest. A slightly lower rate can matter more on long terms. If you plan extra principal payments, total interest falls and the loan ends earlier—this basic tool does not model extra payments, but it still helps you set a baseline payment.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include taxes and insurance?

No. This estimates principal and interest only. Your full housing payment may also include property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, and PMI.

Is this the same as a mortgage quote?

No. Lenders use their own rates, fees, and underwriting. Use this for a planning estimate only.

What about adjustable-rate mortgages?

This tool assumes a fixed rate for the full term. ARMs change over time and are not modeled here.

Should I enter the home price or the loan amount?

Enter the amount you expect to borrow (home price minus down payment), not the full purchase price, unless you are financing 100%.

Why does a shorter term raise the monthly payment?

You repay the same principal in fewer months, so each payment must be larger—but you usually pay much less total interest.

Are results guaranteed?

No. Results are educational estimates. Confirm figures with your lender before making a purchase decision.
